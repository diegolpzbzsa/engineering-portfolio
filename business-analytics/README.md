\# IBM AI Strategy and Financial Analysis



\## Overview



This project was my IB Business Management Internal Assessment. I investigated whether IBM's investment in artificial intelligence, particularly its strategy around WatsonX and healthcare applications, had helped improve the company's financial and strategic position.



The project combined financial analysis with business strategy tools. My goal was not only to describe IBM's situation, but to connect financial data with strategic decisions and evaluate what IBM could do next.



\## Research Question



To what extent has IBM improved its financial position through its investment in artificial intelligence in healthcare?



\## Strategic Analysis



I first used a Lewin force-field analysis to compare the main factors supporting and limiting IBM's AI strategy.



!\[Lewin force-field analysis](images/force-field-analysis.png)



The analysis considered opportunities such as AI leadership, diversification, and expansion into other industries, while also accounting for implementation costs, competition, privacy concerns, and the difficulty of integrating AI into healthcare.



\## Financial Analysis



I then analyzed IBM's financial position using data from its balance sheet, income statement, and financial ratios.



!\[Financial ratio analysis](images/financial-ratios.png)



I focused on indicators such as:



\- liquidity

\- profitability

\- return on equity

\- debt levels

\- gross and net margins



This helped me evaluate whether the strategic shift toward software and AI was consistent with an improving financial position, while also identifying the effect of higher investment and debt.



\## Strategic Recommendations



To connect the financial analysis with IBM's future strategy, I created a cross-SWOT analysis.



!\[Cross-SWOT analysis](images/cross-swot.png)



Instead of only listing strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, I used the matrix to develop possible strategies.



These included expanding AI into additional products and industries, using IBM's consulting network to support implementation, targeting markets better able to absorb high implementation costs, and improving data-security and privacy practices.



\## What I Learned



This project helped me understand how business decisions should be evaluated from more than one perspective.



A strategy can create growth opportunities while also increasing costs, debt, and implementation risk. Because of this, I learned to connect financial indicators with strategic decisions instead of treating them as separate parts of a business.



It also gave me experience turning data into a recommendation, which is different from simply calculating a ratio or identifying a trend.
