\# Physics Extended Essay



\## Energy Transfer in a Drum



This project was my IB Physics Extended Essay, an independent research project in which I investigated how the tension of a drum membrane affects the efficiency of energy transfer through the system.



The main challenge was turning a theoretical problem involving vibration, wave behavior, and energy transfer into an experiment that I could actually measure.



\## Research Question



How does the efficiency of energy transmission in a drum vary as a function of membrane tension?



\## Experimental Setup



I designed a repeatable setup in which a drumstick rotated from a fixed initial position and struck the membrane under controlled conditions.



!\[Experimental setup](images/experimental-setup.png)



The goal was to keep the impact conditions as consistent as possible while changing the membrane tension.



\## Measuring the Input Energy



I used video analysis software to track the motion of the drumstick and estimate the mechanical energy transferred into the system.



!\[Tracker analysis of striker motion](images/striker-tracker-analysis.png)



This allowed me to connect the experimental setup with the energy calculations rather than treating the input force as an unknown quantity.



\## Measuring the Membrane Response



To estimate the amplitude of the vibration, I placed a liquid-filled container on the drum and analyzed the resulting motion using Tracker.



!\[Tracker analysis of membrane amplitude](images/membrane-amplitude-tracker.png)



The liquid also made the vibration patterns easier to observe, which allowed me to identify changes in the vibration modes as membrane tension changed.



!\[Observed vibration mode](images/vibration-mode-pattern.png)



\## Physics and Modeling



The analysis required combining several areas of physics:



\- wave behavior in circular membranes

\- resonance and natural frequency

\- Bessel functions and vibration modes

\- rotational motion of the striking mechanism

\- mechanical energy transfer

\- uncertainty and experimental limitations



One of the most important parts of the project was understanding that changing the tension did not only change the frequency. It could also change the vibration mode of the membrane, which had a significant effect on the energy transferred through the system.



\## Results



The experimental results showed that the efficiency of energy transfer increased as membrane tension increased.



!\[Energy-transfer efficiency vs membrane tension](images/efficiency-vs-tension.png)



The relationship was not perfectly simple because changes in tension also affected frequency, amplitude, and vibration mode. The largest changes in efficiency appeared when the system moved between different vibration modes.



At the highest measured tension, the calculated energy-transfer efficiency was approximately 9.3%, while at the lowest measured tension it was approximately 1.0%.



\## What I Learned



This project changed how I approach technical problems. I initially thought the main challenge would be finding the correct equations, but the experiment made me realize that the harder part is often connecting a model to a real physical system.



I had to decide what could actually be measured, understand the assumptions behind the model, deal with experimental limitations, and adjust the analysis when the real behavior did not match the ideal case perfectly.



That process is one of the reasons I became increasingly interested in experimental engineering, controls, and systems where modeling has to be connected with physical behavior.
