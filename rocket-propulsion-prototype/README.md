\# Experimental Rocket Propulsion Prototype



\## Overview



This project involved the design, development, and physical testing of an experimental rocket propulsion prototype.



What interested me most about the project was the transition from an initial engineering idea into a system that had to work physically. The design process required us to make decisions based on physics and engineering constraints, but testing showed problems and limitations that were difficult to predict from the design alone.



\## My Contribution



My work included contributing to the design, development, and testing of the prototype.



As the project progressed, I had to connect theoretical decisions with the behavior of the physical system, analyze what happened during testing, and help determine what needed to change before the next iteration.



\## Design and Development



The project began by defining the main requirements and translating them into a physical design.



This required thinking about how the different components interacted as a system rather than treating each part independently.




\## Testing and Iteration



Physical testing was one of the most important parts of the project.



The first design did not simply behave exactly as expected. Testing exposed limitations that required us to analyze the results, reconsider parts of the design, and make changes before testing again.




\## What I Learned



This project made me understand the difference between designing something theoretically and making a real system work.



A model can help predict behavior, but a physical system introduces manufacturing limitations, measurement uncertainty, unexpected interactions, and other effects that may not appear in the initial design.



Because of this, I learned to treat testing as part of the engineering process rather than simply as a final confirmation that the design works.
